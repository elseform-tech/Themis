Start subagents for independent parts of the task. Put who you are starting and why in `say`. Each one runs on its own with only its brief. Give each one `write_paths`: the project-relative folders (ending in `/`) or files, never globs, that it alone may create or change, or `[]` when it only reads; one that builds anything needs the paths it builds. Two running subagents can't share a path, and `.themis/` and `.git/` are never allowed. The brief must be self-contained: what to look at, what to report, and in what form. This returns their ids at once and you keep working. Each subagent's result arrives later as a note, before your next step; call wait_subagents only when you have nothing else to do. A subagent that got something wrong can be corrected with message_subagent rather than started again. Good uses: reviewing or investigating several areas at once, searches across a large codebase. Give write paths for parallel work on separate areas, such as tests in one folder and docs in another. A subagent sees the same skills you do: when one applies to its part, such as one about testing first, name it in the brief. Not for a single small edit or anything that needs the conversation.
